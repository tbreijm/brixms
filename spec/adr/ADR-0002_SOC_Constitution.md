Reijm, 2026-07-25. **SOC expands to Settlement-Oriented Computing**: settlement is the organizing primitive the way objects are in OOP (`OOP : Java :: SOC : Brix`). The lax/tight split is what `Audited` and the audit-factorization authority exist for; the global axiom is not reinstated by the terminology clause.

## 1. Thesis (supersedes ADR-0001 §1)

BrixMS has **one category**. Its objects are *configurations*; its arrows are
*witnesses* between configurations. A software system, a simulation of it, the
model it implements, and the world fragment it is coupled to are **not four
primitive kinds of thing** — they are configurations, and the relationships
between them are witnesses. There is **no schema/instance primitive**:
"instance-of" is derived as the carrier of an identity witness
($\carrier{A}=\{x\mid(x,x)\in\rho_{\id_A}\}$), and "has-type" is *one
realization judgment under one regime among many*, not a distinguished
ontological relation.

Three structures sit on this one category:

1. **Realization** is a *normal lax functor* $R:\mathbf{SOC}\to\Rel$,
   $R(A)=\carrier A$, $R(w)=\rho_w$. Laxity
   ($\rho_{g\circ f}\supseteq\rho_g\circ\rho_f$) is deliberate and
   one-directional: **whatever a chain realizes, the composite realizes** —
   compressed/compiled paths are *sound* (the compiler direction). A *tight*
   subcategory $\mathcal K$ generated by a specified class $\mathcal G$ of
   primitive **logged** witnesses gives *exact* decomposition
   $k=g_n\circ\cdots\circ g_1$ with $\rho_k=\rho_{g_n}\circ\cdots\circ\rho_{g_1}$
   for every committed settlement (the audit direction). One axiom gives
   soundness everywhere; one earned subcategory plus one decomposition
   certificate give accountability where it is owed.

2. **Dynamics** is a *coalgebra* on execution configurations
   $e=\langle x,p,h\rangle$ (world, policy, history). Candidates come from
   realization filtered by an admissibility judgment $\mathsf{Adm}$; a
   *settlement discipline* $\mathsf D$ packages the observed successor set into a
   fixed **behavior functor** $F_O$; a **keyed calendar** determinizes it via a
   natural transformation $\mathsf{select}_K:B^{\mathrm{uk}}_{K,O}\Rightarrow D_O$.
   **Deliberation is plural; commitment is singular.**

3. **Saturation** distinguishes *administrative* steps ($\tau$) from *realizing*
   steps ($o\in O$) and is **divergence-sensitive**: it hides finite
   administrative prefixes but never identifies quiescence with infinite
   administrative search. The **universal world** (a faithful $F_O$-self-image)
   is a *conjecture* requiring a **settlement interface** (a total one-step
   procedure returning the full $F_O$-structure, including explicit quiescence
   certificates). The saturation layer and that interface are pinned by
   [ADR-0014](./ADR-0014_Divergence_Sensitive_Saturation.md).

The distinctive contribution — the reason this is *a different perspective on
reality* — is unchanged from ADR-0001 and re-anchored here: BrixMS carries
**epistemic status, cost, and provenance as one fabric**. SOC supplies the
ontology and the operational semantics; the epistemic lattice (ADR-0001 §4,
carried forward §4 below) supplies the grading. The two compose (§5).

## 3. Decision D1 (reframed) — Two kernels over one configuration/witness substrate

There remain **exactly two trusted kernels**; they share **one substrate** and
depend on neither each other nor the resolver/regime stack.

| Kernel | SOC role | Question it answers | Depends on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Settlement kernel** (`soc-core` committed coalgebra; `brix-oracle` reborn as reference) | the committed $D_O$-coalgebra $\gamma=\mathsf{select}_K\circ\delta$ + audit factorization over $\mathcal G$ | *Which settlement did the calendar commit at this step, and what is its logged tight decomposition?* | canonical artifacts |
| **Dependent proof kernel** (`brix-kernel`, unchanged role) | certifies propositions *about* the configuration/witness/behavior structure | *Does this canonical explicit term prove this proposition in this exact context?* | **only** `brix-semantic` |

The substrate — SOC configurations, witnesses, regimes, decompositions, and the
epistemic artifacts — lives in **`brix-semantic`** (extended, §6), depending
**only on `brix-canon`**. The proof kernel's new proof targets are named in the
proof-debt items (§8): decomposition validity, governor monotonicity, and
counter-machine trace correspondence.

The **naive recompute-the-world loop is deliberately retained** as the settlement
kernel's *reference oracle* (`brix-oracle`'s role reborn — SOC §"Settlement");
the fast engine is differential-tested against it. This is the anti-v1 discipline
made structural (§9).

## 4. Decision D2 (extended) — The single epistemic outcome lattice

Carried forward from ADR-0001 §4, **extended by exactly one member**:
`Audited`. SOC introduces exactly one new structural distinction — lax vs
tight — and the lattice mirrors it with exactly one new outcome; anything more
is inflation, anything less forces the audit-factorization result into a wrong
home (the ADR-0001 five-member lattice made "checker-verified tight
decomposition" either an overclaimed `Proven` or an underclaimed `Derived`).
Defined once, in `brix-semantic`, frozen.

```
                 Proven            Refuted
                    \               /
                     \             /
                    Audited (tight decomposition replay-verified; not a theorem)
                        |
                   Derived (revision-local, settlement-authoritative)
                        |
                    Measured / Estimated
                        |
                     Unknown            ← bottom; never collapses to true/false
```

- **`Audited`** — the committed step's tight $\mathcal G$-decomposition
  $k=g_n\circ\cdots\circ g_1$ has been **replayed and verified to compose
  exactly** (SOC audit factorization, discharged for this witness). Stronger
  than `Derived` (which asserts only that the engine committed the step, on a
  compact support record); strictly weaker than `Proven` (still interpreted
  over the settlement world, **`is_theorem` remains kernel-poles-only**). An
  `Audited` judgement pins the exact scheduled trace — the calendar keys are
  part of the decomposition — so the schedule-dependence of the `Derived`
  frontier (§5) is *recorded*, not ambient.
- Ordinals are canonical ABI (append-only, never reordered): `Proven` 0,
  `Refuted` 1, `Derived` 2, `Measured` 3, `Unknown` 4 — as encoded today in
  `crates/brix-semantic/src/outcome.rs` — and **`Audited` appends as ordinal
  5**. The lattice partial order is an **explicit function**, never the enum
  declaration/derive order (which already diverges: `Proven`/`Refuted` are
  declaration-adjacent but lattice-incomparable).
- Resource exhaustion is `Unknown`, never `Refuted`. Fail closed to `Unknown`,
  never silently to `Proven` **or `Audited`** — a replay that does not complete
  exactly yields `Unknown(reason)`, never a downgrade-hiding pass.
- **One authority per outcome route.**

**Marked decision point ⟨D-AUD⟩ — RATIFIED (2026-07-25, maintainer).** The
five-member lattice was blessed 2026-07-24 and is live in `outcome.rs`; adding
`Audited` is an append-only ABI extension (new ordinal 5, new authority row =
the audit-factorization checker / reference replayer, no re-encoding of existing
judgements). Ratified together with ⟨D-FO⟩. `outcome.rs` now gains the sixth
member `Audited` (ordinal 5) with its authority row and an explicit lattice
partial-order function; `is_theorem` stays kernel-poles-only.

### 4.1 Verifier-authority table (frozen; one row added)

| Outcome | Sole authority | Nobody else may publish it |
|---|---|---|
| `Proven` / `Refuted` | `brix-kernel` acceptance judgement | regimes may *construct candidates*, never assert acceptance |
| `Audited` | the **audit-factorization checker** — the reference replayer (`brix-oracle` role), replaying a `Decomposition` against the log | the engine's hot loop may *record* decompositions, never assert their verification |
| `Derived` | settlement kernel (committed coalgebra; `brix-oracle` reference) | — |
| `Measured` / `Estimated` | the named external driver / simulator, via a certified-result envelope | — |
| `Unknown` | any regime may *emit* `Unknown(reason)`; no one may *downgrade* a stronger outcome to hide a failure | — |

The `Audited` row makes the two-implementation discipline epistemically
load-bearing: the fast engine publishes `Derived` from the hot path; only the
reference replayer's exact re-composition upgrades it. Differential testing
*is* an outcome upgrade, not a test-only activity.

## 5. How the lattice composes with SOC (the new joint)

This is the load-bearing new content: the epistemic half of ADR-0001 and the
operational half of SOC are **one system**, joined at these points.

1. **A committed calendar step is `Derived`; its replay-verified decomposition
   is `Audited`.** The commitment is `select_K`-selected and authoritative
   *within its revision*; the hot loop records a compact support record plus
   the (unverified) `Decomposition` (§6) — this is SOC's "history stores $c$'s
   primitive tight decomposition." When the audit-factorization checker replays
   that decomposition and verifies exact relational composition, the upgraded
   judgement is `Audited`. The two are **different judgements** (the evidence
   differs, so the `JudgementId` differs), linked by a `Dependency` edge; the
   lax/tight split of the ontology is thereby graded in the lattice.
2. **A kernel certificate about behavior is `Proven`.** Certificates target
   *extensional* facts about the $F_O$-coalgebra (invariants, refinements,
   decomposition validity). `Proven`/`Refuted` are revision-invariant precisely
   because SOC's behavior map factors through the final $F_O$-coalgebra.
   **The elaboration boundary is typed by the lattice:** only `Audited`-supported
   settlement evidence may enter an `elaboration-boundary` edge — an unaudited
   commit is not even a certified rule-match chain, let alone a theorem. The
   canonical upgrade path is `Derived → Audited → Proven`: engine commit,
   oracle replay, kernel elaboration — each step a new judgement, never a
   downgrade.
3. **Divergence-sensitivity is "`Unknown` never collapses."** SOC's refusal to
   identify quiescence with administrative divergence *is* the lattice's refusal
   to collapse `Unknown` to `Refuted`/`false`. A search that has not terminated
   has proved nothing; a quiescence certificate (settlement interface) is what
   upgrades "no successor found yet" to a decided negative.
4. **Realization regimes = the `Measured`/candidate frontier.** Regimes propose;
   only the calendar commits (`Derived`) and only the kernel certifies
   (`Proven`). Exotic/heuristic regimes that exhaust budget land on `Unknown`
   (§9), never on a stronger outcome.
5. **Governance monotonicity is a lattice-safe operation.** Tightening
   $\mathsf{Adm}$ shrinks `cand(e)` pointwise (SOC "Governance monotonicity");
   it can only *remove* candidates, never fabricate a stronger outcome — the
   conservation law of §9's gate.

## 6. Decision D3 (extended) — Canonical artifact identities in SOC terms

All content-addressed, all in `brix-semantic`, all with versioned encoders. The
ADR-0001 §5 identities are preserved; the SOC-specific artifacts are added.

**Preserved from ADR-0001 §5 (unchanged):**

- **`ContextId`** (§6.1 below) — with the **frozen root-digest invariant**.
- **`PropositionId`** — the canonical domain statement (named `Proposition`, not
  `Claim`).
- **`EvidenceId`** — with the **durability axis** governing retraction: *durable*
  = `KernelCertificate`, `KernelRefutation`, **and tight `Decomposition`s closed
  over their context**; *revision-scoped* = `GroundAssertion`,
  `SettlementReplay`, `Measurement`/`Simulation`, `Suggestion`,
  `CertifiedExternalResult`.
- **`JudgementId`** = `(ContextId, PropositionId, outcome, EvidenceId)` —
  **search-invariant**.
- **`Dependency`** — one artifact, typed edge kinds incl. the structural
  **`elaboration-boundary`** (settlement support becomes `Proven` evidence
  *only* across it, and only from an **`Audited`** judgement — §4, §5.2).
- **`DiscoveryRun`** — search strategy/history, deliberately **outside**
  `Judgement` (validity invariant under search strategy and history).
- **Cost** — folded into propositions; a per-step emitted cost record is
  mandatory and may be `UnknownCost(reason)` but is never omitted or defaulted
  to zero.

**New SOC artifacts (extend `brix-semantic`):**

- **`Witness`** — the identity of a witness $w:A\to B$; carries its `RegimeId`.
  Configurations reuse existing canonical value identities; a witness is a typed
  correspondence, not a new value kind.
- **`RegimeId`** — the identity of a realization regime (literal equality,
  structural, entailment, probabilistic-threshold, …). Names *which* $\rho_w$
  interpretation a witness carries. `has-type` is one regime among many.
- **Generator registry `𝒢`** — the specified class of primitive **logged**
  settlement witnesses that generates the tight subcategory $\mathcal K$. Each
  generator has a canonical identity; membership is data, not convention.
- **`Decomposition`** — evidence artifact recording a finite factorization
  $k=g_n\circ\cdots\circ g_1$ with $g_i\in\mathcal G$ and the intermediate
  configurations $x_0,\dots,x_n$. Recorded (unverified) by the hot loop as part
  of a committed step's `Derived` evidence (§5.1); once the audit-factorization
  checker replays it and verifies exact relational composition, it supports the
  upgraded `Audited` judgement (§4) — and only in that verified form may it
  cross an `elaboration-boundary` edge (§5.2). Its durability classification
  (§2, "closed over context") applies to the **verified** form.
- **`Realizes(w,x,y)`** — a `Proposition` kind asserting $x\realizes{w}y$. Makes
  realization judgments first-class statements the kernel and regimes reason
  about.

### 6.1 `ContextId` root-digest invariant (frozen — MUST, carried verbatim)

> The **root** context — `root snapshot + program + empty assumptions + default
> profile + default limits` — MUST canonically encode to a digest **equal to
> today's `ScopeId::root()` digest** (`crates/brix-ir/src/reflect.rs`,
> PR3.5/#68). This is the hinge that lets `brix.type` gain real scoped contexts
> without breaking its `FactId`-for-`FactId` shadow parity. It requires a golden
> vector.

This invariant is unchanged by SOC and is re-frozen here explicitly. The
`brix.type` structural regime (semantic stage 4) re-enters through exactly this
anchor; the golden vector is a stage-1 gate (§ Build Plan v3, step S1).

## 7. Realization regimes (reframed resolvers)

A **realization regime** (formerly "resolver": `brix.type`, `brix.proof`,
`brix.complexity`, compatibility, authorization, …) is an ordinary sealed BrixMS
package presenting a class of witnesses under one $\rho_w$ interpretation. As a
running engine component a regime is a **dataflow operator**: `footprint()` +
`apply(delta) → candidate delta` (§9). A regime may **search, rank, derive
candidates, and construct certificates**; it may **never** publish
`Proven`/`Refuted` (only `brix-kernel`) nor `Derived` (only the calendar).

The **committed core** — generators $\mathcal G$ + admissibility guards — is a
*restricted, analyzable* language compiled to indexed joins with filter
push-down; its optimization is *licensed by the governance-monotonicity theorem*
(§5.5). **Exotic/heuristic regimes** run in deliberation, budgeted, and land on
`Unknown` when budgets exhaust — **they pay at proposal, never at commit.**

## 8. Frozen-pending-ratification: the behavior signature $(O, F_O)$

SOC's adequacy is only well-typed once $O$ (the observation set) and $F_O$ (the
behavior functor) are **fixed, versioned, and frozen alongside the lattice**.
This is a new load-bearing ABI decision. It is **not yet ratified**; the
candidates and the decision point are stated here.

### 8.1 The commitment/deliberation split (settled)

Independent of the $F_O$ choice, SOC fixes the *architecture*: deliberation
lives in the unique-key branching functor $B^{\mathrm{uk}}_{K,O}$, and
$\mathsf{select}_K:B^{\mathrm{uk}}_{K,O}\Rightarrow D_O$ commits the least-key
candidate. $K=(\text{phase/time},\ \text{priority},\ \text{canonical-digest
tie-break})$; the final component is a unique tie-break, so selection is total
and deterministic. **This split is ratified.** The open question is only *which
$F_O$ the committed coalgebra targets.*

### 8.2 Candidate committed functors $F_O$

| # | $F_O(X)$ | Character | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A (recommended default)** | $D_O(X)=1+O\times X$ | partial **deterministic** committed behavior | Directly the codomain of $\mathsf{select}_K$; commitment is singular by construction; simplest final coalgebra; safety = one-step closure. Quiescence = the `1` summand. Deliberation may still be plural in $B^{\mathrm{uk}}$. **Matches "commitment is singular" with least machinery.** |
| B | $\mathcal P_f(O\times X)$ | finite **nondeterministic** committed behavior | Keeps may-nondeterminism *at commit*; gives clean may-trace monotonicity (SOC governance prop). But it discards the calendar's singular-commit theorem and needs a heavier weak-bisimulation story; harder to reconcile with append-only deterministic replay (§9). |
| C | $\mathcal D_{\le 1,f}(O\times X)$ | finite-support **subprobabilistic** committed behavior | Commit distributions; needed only if committed steps are inherently stochastic. Requires a weight discipline in $\mathsf D$; set-monotonicity (§5.5) is replaced by quantitative monotonicity in the native order (SOC scheduling caveat). Defer unless a regime demands it. |

**Recommendation (for ratification):** adopt **Candidate A**, $F_O=D_O=1+O\times
X$ committed, with $B^{\mathrm{uk}}_{K,O}$ deliberative. Nondeterministic or
quantitative committed behavior (B/C) is a *future versioned $F_O$*, admitted
only behind a new behavior-signature version tag, never by silent widening.

### 8.3 The observation set $O$

$O$ must be effectively encodable (SOC "Fixed effective behavior signature").
Two candidate granularities, **decision point open**:

- **$O_{\min}$ (lean):** $O$ = a small finite set of settlement-event *tags* —
  the committed outcome class + a digest of the committed `JudgementId`. Cheap to
  encode; keeps the behavior alphabet stable across regimes; richer detail lives
  off-band in the (retractable) history, not in the observed behavior.
- **$O_{\text{rich}}$ (projective):** each observation carries a canonical
  projection of the committed `Judgement` (proposition digest + outcome +
  evidence-kind). More expressive bisimulations and a more informative
  behavior map, at the cost of a larger frozen alphabet and heavier equality.

**Marked decision point ⟨D-FO⟩ — RATIFIED (2026-07-25, maintainer).** $(O, F_O)$
is fixed on the presented default: **$F_O=D_O=1+O\times X$ committed** (partial
deterministic committed behavior — Candidate A of §8.2), **$O=O_{\min}$**
(settlement-event tags: the committed outcome class + a digest of the committed
`JudgementId`), with deliberation in $B^{\mathrm{uk}}_{K,O}$. This is the
behavior signature `soc-core` encoders freeze against. It is versioned exactly
like the canon vectors and the outcome ordinals: append-only, changed only by a
new signature version tag plus a golden vector. Candidates B/C (nondeterministic
/ subprobabilistic committed behavior) remain future versioned $F_O$s, admitted
only behind a new signature version tag, never by silent widening.

## 9. Engineering doctrine (normative — the anti-v1 constitution)

v1 was a ~250-line JavaScript recompute-the-world loop with cartesian-product
candidate generation and per-candidate deep copies (thesis ch. 6). This ADR
forbids that architecture at the level of a conformance invariant.

### 9.1 THE invariant (MUST)

> **Cost per committed step MUST be $\propto |\Delta|\times(\text{index
> fanout})$, and MUST NOT be $\propto |\text{world}|$.** Doubling the number of
> *inert* configurations MUST NOT change per-step cost.

This is enforced as a **conformance/benchmark gate from the start** (Build Plan
v3 step E5, the *O(Δ) gate*), instrumented by the ADR-0001 stage-4a cost
records. A change that regresses it is a red build, not a later optimization.

### 9.2 Normative implementation rules

- **Interning.** Canonical digests are interned to dense `u32` handles; the hot
  loop uses handles only. Digests are computed at boundaries (commit, evidence,
  replay), never per candidate in the inner loop.
- **State.** Persistent HAMT-style maps with structural sharing. The history
  digest is a chain: $h'=H(h_{\text{digest}}, \text{step})$, O(1) per step.
- **Candidates are a materialized incremental view** (semi-naive, delta-driven),
  never a re-run query. The regime trait is a dataflow operator:
  `footprint()` / `apply(delta) → candidate delta`.
- **Calendar** = priority queue keyed by $K=(\text{phase/time}, \text{priority},
  \text{canonical-digest tie-break})$; least key commits (SOC $\mathsf{select}_K$).
- **Compact support in the hot loop.** Record compact `Support(edge, rule,
  match)` records; expand the full tight `SettlementStep`/`Decomposition` **lazily
  off the hot path** (lax direction = compiler license; tight direction =
  auditors).
- **Expressiveness split.** Committed core ($\mathcal G$ + guards) in a
  restricted analyzable language compiled to indexed joins with filter
  push-down (licensed by governance monotonicity, §5.5). Exotic/heuristic
  regimes run budgeted in deliberation and land on `Unknown` on exhaustion —
  they pay at proposal, never at commit.
- **Parallelism.** Sharded deliberation + parallel min-reduce for the least key
  (naturality of $\mathsf{select}_K$ makes this sound); independence batching via
  read/write footprints (the #140 role-fragment extractor machinery, reused as
  the footprint feed); optimistic speculation governed by SOC's
  scheduling-stability caveat (removing the least-key candidate may reselect —
  speculation must be revocable).
- **Reference oracle.** The naive recompute-the-world loop is *deliberately*
  reimplemented as the reference oracle; the fast engine is differential-tested
  against it (SOC settlement discipline, made testable).

### 9.3 Engineering build order (structure, not timeline)

`(E1)` interning + persistent store + chained digest → `(E2)` naive oracle loop
→ `(E3)` delta protocol + regime trait → `(E4)` indexed incremental candidates +
calendar heap → **`(E5)` the O(Δ) benchmark gate — before any further
optimization** → `(E6)` compact support + lazy audit expansion → `(E7)` footprint
batching + parallel deliberation → `(E8)` guard/rule compilation (bytecode first;
JIT only if profiles demand). This order is interleaved with the semantic
sequence in [`Build_Plan_v3_SOC.md`](../Build_Plan_v3_SOC.md).

  fail-closed-to-`Unknown` discipline; not something to "fix."

## 11. Non-goals

- **Not** re-litigating the frozen ADR-0001 §§4–5–7 decisions. The single
  exception is the append-only `Audited` extension (⟨D-AUD⟩, §4) — an addition,
  not a re-decision: existing members, ordinals, and authority rows are
  untouched.
- The proof kernel's TCB **excludes** parsing, elaboration, proof search,
  tactics, metavariables, implicit holes, general recursion, provenance ranking,
  candidate generation, and calendar selection.
- **Not** enlarging `brix.type` into a universal library — it is *one structural
  regime*, entering at semantic stage 4, not the ontology.
- **Not** widening $F_O$ silently — B/C in §8.2 require a new versioned behavior
  signature.
- The lax direction is **not** required to be tight; only committed
  $\mathcal G$-witnesses carry exact decompositions.

## 12. Relationship to existing work

- The **`brix-semantic` crate** (exists, 704 lines, ADR-0001 stage 1) is
  **kept and extended** per §6.
- The **old engine stack** (`brix-ast`, `brix-ir`, `brixc`, `brix-rt`,
  `brix-phase`, `reflect.rs`/`infer.rs`) is retained **only as a differential
  oracle** during transition, then retired; internals are **not** ported.
- **`brix-oracle`'s role is reborn** as the reference implementation of the
  committed coalgebra (§3).
- **Kept:** `brix-canon`, `brix-semantic`, `brix-conformance` (corpus +
  differential harness).
- **New crate:** `soc-core` (configuration/witness stores + realization
  interface + $\mathsf{Adm}$ + candidate enumeration + calendar/commit).
- The **#140 role-fragment extractor** is valuable now only as the
  read/write **footprint feed** for independence batching (§9.2).
- **#15** (self-hosting) is **closed history**; its successor is **PD-2 / CJ-1**.

### One-line summary

One category of configurations and witnesses; realization is a **lax** functor
into `Rel` (sound compression) with a **tight** audit-generated subcategory
(exact decomposition); dynamics is a **settlement coalgebra** determinized by a
**keyed calendar** into a fixed, versioned behavior functor $F_O$; the
**epistemic lattice** grades its outcomes (committed step = `Derived`,
replay-verified decomposition = `Audited`, kernel certificate = `Proven`,
unterminated search = `Unknown`); and the whole thing is
built under **one non-negotiable invariant — cost per step $\propto|\Delta|$,
never $\propto|\text{world}|$.**
